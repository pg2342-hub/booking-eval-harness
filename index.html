Booking Eval Harness

A runnable 25-case eval for an AI booking agent at a home services contractor. It checks that every AI-booked job lands with the right job type, fee, service area and slot, and that the agent hands off the calls it should never book.

Code on GitHub

The point. A booking counts as a win even when a detail is wrong: a plan member quoted the $95 standard fee instead of the $49 member fee is still a "booking." This harness defines what a correct booking is for one contractor and the gate an agent must pass before it books on its own: 24 of 25 cases on all three runs, with zero critical errors.

The reference agent reads structured facts, not the raw transcript, so its 25 of 25 shows the checks can be written as code and the grader agrees with a correct policy. Does each check have cases that catch it?

An eval set that still passes with a check switched off is not testing that check. Here the reference agent runs with one check off at a time.

Check switched offCases passedCases with a critical errorCases that caught it
Job check23 of 2509, 11
Fee check22 of 25010, 13, 17
Area check23 of 25214, 23
Slot check6 of 25019 cases
Hand-off rule22 of 25322, 24, 25

The job and area checks are caught by only two cases each, the thinnest coverage and the first place to add cases. With the slot check off, the agent takes the first open window, which is the emergency window, so almost every routine booking fails.

Error types

CodeWhat went wrongSeverity
E1Wrong job type, business unit, or campaignHigh
E2Wrong fee, membership missed, or billing contact wrongHigh
E3Booked outside the service area or in the wrong marketCritical
E4Wrong slot or priority, or a duplicate jobHigh
E5Should have handed off and did notCritical
E6Flagged, refused, or handed off a job it could have bookedLow

The rule when a check is unsure

Book and flag when a CSR can fix the error before the technician leaves (a lapsed membership, an unclear job type, an unconfirmed promotion). Do not book, and hand off, when it cannot be fixed later (an out-of-area address, a gas smell, a commercial job, a billing dispute). Stopping a booking costs the job, especially after hours; booking the wrong one costs a truck roll or a safety risk.
